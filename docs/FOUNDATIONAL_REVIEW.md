## Document 1: Human Narrative Atlas (HNA), 19 pages

**Core idea:** represent an audiovisual work through a multimodal narrative model, described as a narrative digital twin (pp. 1-2).

Its fifteen phases cover transcription and linguistic statistics (pp. 2-3); semantics (pp. 3-5); emotion (pp. 5-6); intentions (pp. 6-7); audio (pp. 7-8); visible entities (pp. 8-9); color (pp. 9-10); settings (pp. 10-11); clothing (pp. 11-12); symbols (pp. 12-13); characters (p. 13); narrative structure (p. 14); a combined graph (pp. 14-15); culture (pp. 15-16); and final indices (pp. 16-17). Outputs include maps, graphs, timelines, interpretations, comparisons, and a structured dataset (pp. 17-18).

**What to preserve:** time alignment, multiple modalities, structured outputs, traceable character relationships, and the intention to make analysis reproducible.

**What needs revision:** the ambition to represent everything a work communicates, explicitly and implicitly (p. 19), has no defined completeness criterion. Observable word counts and interpretive philosophical-depth scores cannot be treated as equivalent measurements. Emotional cues in a film do not directly measure viewers' emotions. The proposed 0-100 scores have no supplied calibration procedure.

**Role in the project:** a possible measurement and annotation instrument. It can support research without needing to capture a whole work exhaustively.

## Document 2: Narrative Dynamics Engine (NDE), 20 pages

**Core idea:** extend narrative analysis into cognition, behavior, and social dynamics across film, books, politics, religion, advertising, news, and other domains (pp. 1-3).

The fifteen phases propose narrative structures (pp. 3-4), cognitive vectors (pp. 4-5), belief graphs (pp. 5-6), propagation (pp. 6-7), energy (pp. 7-8), inertia (pp. 8-9), entropy (p. 9), control theory (pp. 10-11), simulations (pp. 11-12), economic dynamics (pp. 12-13), political dynamics (pp. 13-14), cultural dynamics (pp. 14-15), a social digital twin (p. 15), prediction (p. 16), and comparisons across domains (pp. 16-17). The closing pages propose Computational Narrative Dynamics and Unified Theory of Narrative Systems as possible disciplinary names (pp. 19-20).

**What to preserve:** explicit temporal questions, uncertainty in predictions, hypotheses connecting narrative features to outcomes, and attention to networks and feedback.

**What needs revision:** a content-derived cognitive vector is not a measurement of a population's cognition. The equation `x(t+1) = f(x,u)` is a model template, not an identified predictive system. The proposed energy product has no justified functional form or normalization. A simulation's internal consistency does not establish its accuracy about people.

**Role in the project:** a future modeling layer, contingent on independently observed outcomes and baseline comparisons.

## Document 3: Narrative Systems Engineering (NSE), 24 pages

**Core idea:** turn the preceding ideas into a discipline concerned with narrative systems, their analysis, simulation, design, and optimization (pp. 1-2).

The proposed foundational paper has twelve sections: manifesto (pp. 2-3); definitions (pp. 4-5); axioms (pp. 5-6); mathematics (pp. 6-7); branches (pp. 7-8); metrics (pp. 8-10); methodology (pp. 10-11); modular architecture (pp. 11-12); applications (pp. 12-13); ethics (pp. 13-14); a 2026-2050 agenda (p. 14); and concluding vision (p. 15). It then requests another academic-paper version (pp. 15-16).

A separate master roadmap titled 2026-2036 follows: foundation in months 0-3 (pp. 16-17), intellectual-priority activities in months 3-6 (p. 18), theory in months 6-12 (pp. 19-20), validation in years 1-2 (pp. 20-22), a lab in years 2-3 (p. 22), and initial software in years 3-4 (p. 23). The supplied file ends on an effectively blank p. 24; it does not supply milestones through 2036 despite that title.

**What to preserve:** the request for formal definitions, explicit methodology, modular organization, and attention to governance and misuse.

**What needs revision:** scientific claims should be tested before being promoted to universal axioms. A founding paper's length and formal tone cannot establish novelty. Validation should begin immediately, and small research scripts should accompany it rather than wait three years. Naming branches does not establish their scientific independence.

**Role in the project:** an umbrella research ambition and a source of future questions, not a validated charter to adopt wholesale.

## Evolution and recommended integration

| Stage | Main object | Desired result | Main unresolved requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| HNA | Audiovisual work | Structured representation | Reliable annotation and bounded coverage |
| NDE | Narrative interacting with people over time | Prediction and simulation | Observed outcomes and identifiable models |
| NSE | Research program spanning these layers | A field and engineering practice | Distinct contribution and independent validation |

Our proposed integration keeps HNA as an instrument, NDE as a possible model family, and NSE as a provisional program name. This is our organizing interpretation, not a structure already validated by the PDFs.

The clearest first bridge is from a bounded representation to an independently measurable task. Later bridges can test reception, persistence, and diffusion separately. A successful first bridge would not automatically validate the later ones.
